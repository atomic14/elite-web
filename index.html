HARMLESS

A browser tribute to Elite (1984).

Harmless is the combat rating every commander starts at. The ladder ends, for almost nobody, at E L I T E.

Eight galaxies. 2,048 worlds, every one of them generated by the same arithmetic Braben and Bell used on a 32K BBC Micro. You start docked at Lave with a Cobra Mk III, a hundred credits and a rating of Harmless. What happens next is entirely your problem.

The original galaxy, byte for byte

The 1984 galaxy was never stored. It was calculated on every boot, from a three-word seed run through the same shift-and-add. That algorithm is reproduced here byte for byte, which is why Lave is still system 7, still Rich Agricultural, still a Dictatorship at tech level 5, and why Diso still has 4.1 billion Black Furry Felines on it.

The market model is the original's too: seventeen commodities whose prices slide with the local economy, so agricultural worlds sell food cheaply and industrial worlds pay for it. Every planet description is generated by the original's recursive "goat soup" grammar.

The pirates learned to fly, and we threw it away

The ships that hunt you used to be a neural network trained by neuroevolution self-play: thousands of generations of pirates and traders shooting at each other in a headless simulator, with the survivors breeding.

It worked, and that was the problem. Evolution found that the best pirate is a turret — stop dead, pivot on the spot, hold a firing line. It won every measurement we had and it was miserable to fight. So the ships that hunt you now fly an attack run written by hand: close, pass, extend, come round again.

The trained policies still fly — on your side. The armed trader who fights back, and the combat computer you can buy, are both a neural network that learned deflection shooting, energy management and when to break off, none of which anybody wrote down.

Never played Elite?

Nobody will give you a mission. That is the point, and it is also why the original shipped with a manual and a novella in the box. So does this one.

Flight Training Manual

How to trade, jump, dock and stay alive, with a first run worked out against the game's own market model.

The Long Way Out

An original novella, plus papers from the eight galaxies: GalCop advisories, obituaries, and a dead pilot's log.

What's in it

  • 2,048 worlds across eight galaxies, byte-accurate to 1984
  • Wireframe ships: Cobra, Python, Anaconda, Viper, Thargoid
  • Trading, contracts and piracy, with an economy that reacts to you
  • A living galaxy, where convoys move and trade while you're elsewhere
  • Trained combat AI for pirates, traders and your own defences
  • Inhabitant portraits for all 256 worlds of galaxy one
  • Saves in your browser, named by you. No account, ever.
